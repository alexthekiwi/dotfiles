# Style: lo-fi that looks considered

The wireframe should read as deliberate design thinking, not generated filler. The kit CSS already follows these rules. Keep page-level styles and copy consistent with them.

## Visual

- **Greyscale:** black, greys and white carry everything. States read through words, weight and solid or dashed borders. Yellow belongs only to review notes. Real product colours (swatches, colour charts) are content and keep their colour.
- **Sentence case** for every heading, eyebrow, label, button, badge, table header and menu heading.
- **Even borders:** boxes and messages get the same 1.5px border on every side, and a warning or error is set apart by a grey fill or a heavier border.
- **Logo:** a plain wordmark in sentence case. If the brand guidelines show a shape, mimic that shape only.
- **Hero:** full-bleed. When the brand guidelines or mock-ups lead with imagery, it fills the first screen but stops just short of the fold, so the top of the next section shows. The headline sits large and centred over a labelled image placeholder.
- **Image placeholders:** crossed boxes whose label says what the photo should show ("Group of staff in matching polos, outdoors").
- **Tiles:** large enough to read, three columns by default, wrapping onto more rows rather than squeezing six across.
- **Footer:** full width, with columns split by hairline borders. Each column has a small sentence-case heading and its links, and some columns have a line pinned to the bottom (copyright, social links, legal). Leave out agency credits, and include a market or currency switcher only when the client sells in more than one market at launch.
- **Header dropdowns:** clear chevrons (a 7px bordered chevron that flips when open), not tiny glyphs.
- **Type scale:** 14px body text, with headings stepped down from 29px. Keep wireframe type compact so modules, not text, carry the layout.
- **Rhythm:** consistent spacing, a 4px radius, and sections separated by whitespace or one dashed hairline.

## Copy

- **Voice:** the client's vocabulary from the sources, in NZ English. Use the client's public words ("supplier" when "distributor" is internal).
- **Sentences:** short and declarative. Break clauses with full stops, commas, colons or brackets. An em dash appears only as an empty-cell marker or in a `<title>`.
- **Headings are statements**, such as "Group orders" and "Try a garment first".
- **Specific nouns and numbers** over adjectives. Plain verbs ("choose", "send", "order").
- **Placeholder data** looks real (names, codes, prices) and is labelled as illustrative on the site map.
- **Notes** run to one or two factual sentences. A decision note names its source; a question note names who decides.

## Accessibility

- Every input has a label, and every control is a real button or link.
- Targets are at least 44px, the focus ring is visible, and heading levels are in order.
- Meaning never relies on colour or position alone.
