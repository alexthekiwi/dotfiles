# Alex's Email Voice

This skill ensures all drafted emails match Alex Clark's natural writing voice. Read `references/voice-guide.md` before drafting any email.

## Quick Reference

**Always:**
- Open with "Hey [Name]," ("Hey all," / "Hey both," for groups). Quick mid-thread replies can skip the greeting.
- Do NOT include "Kind Regards," or any signature block. Gmail adds this automatically.
- Use NZ English spelling (organise, recognise, colour, behaviour)
- Write in a warm, conversational but professional tone
- Be direct and clear. Lead with the point.
- End with a specific next step, question, or invitation ("Any q's, just let me know.", "Have a read and let me know what you think.")
- Keep quick replies genuinely short

**Never:**
- **Use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–). Not anywhere, including number ranges.** These are the biggest AI tells. Use commas, full stops, colons, or parentheses, or restructure the sentence. Ranges use a hyphen or words ("2pm to 4pm"). A plain spaced hyphen (" - ") is allowed at most once per email, only as a short casual lead-in like "Sure thing - attached."
- Use "just sing out". Alex has retired it; use "just let me know" instead.
- Use "Dear", "Hello", "Hi there", or overly formal openings
- Use corporate buzzwords (synergy, leverage, circle back, align, loop in)
- Use filler phrases ("I just wanted to reach out", "I hope this email finds you well")
- Sound like AI in other ways. No perfectly structured 5-paragraph essays. No "It's not just X, it's Y" constructions. No reflexive rule-of-three lists. No opening with "I'd be happy to...". No closing with "Let me know if you need anything else!" as a generic catch-all.
- Over-hedge or be excessively cautious in language
- Use "please don't hesitate to" or "don't hesitate to reach out". Alex never says this.
- Use American English spelling

## Process

1. Read `references/voice-guide.md` for the full style breakdown and sample emails
2. Determine the email type (update, proposal, follow-up, quick reply, technical explanation, etc.)
3. Draft the email matching Alex's voice
4. Self-check before presenting:
   - Zero em dashes (—) and zero en dashes (–)? If you've used any, rewrite those sentences.
   - At most one spaced hyphen, and only as a short lead-in?
   - No "sing out"?
   - Reads like Alex from the samples, not like AI?
   - Has the warmth without filler?
   - Ends with a clear next step, question, or invitation?
5. Present the draft using the message_compose tool when available
